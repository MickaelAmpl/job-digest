## 2. Reference sample letters (excerpts <=40 words; rest summarised)

1. MIT CAPD Letter II, undergrad to Deloitte Business Analyst (https://cdn.uconnectlabs.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2025/05/MITCAPD-CoverLetterII.pdf). Opens by naming a conversation with an employee, then a specific firm trait. Strong: ask stated early ("writing to request an invitation to interview for a Business Analyst position"); body 1 = one project with numbers (98 interviews, 6-person team); body 2 = second theme; close links both. Weak: long (~430 words).
2. MIT CAPD Letter IV, master's to Navigant (…CoverLetterIV.pdf). Preview sentence lists 4 strengths in paragraph 1, then each body paragraph proves one (research assistant work, courses, prior consulting jobs). Strong: each paragraph maps to the employer's stated values; ends with a concrete date: "I look forward to speaking with you when you visit the MIT campus on October 10th."
3. UWM Assistant Product Manager (https://uwm.edu/set/wp-content/uploads/sites/631/2026/06/Cover-Letter-Examples.pdf), ~240 words, 4 paras. Closest to target role. Cites: "analyzed sales trends for various product lines, tracked market share... recommended promotion and merchandise strategies". Strong: one-line company-mission hook, then coursework + internship mapped to PM tasks. Weak: generic "creativity" close.
4. UWM KPMG Tax Internship (same PDF). Strong: cites where role was seen (Handshake) + recruiter met at career fair; one data project ("collect and analyze student appointment data, then made recommendations to improve... drop-in advising"). Shows how a non-glamorous job becomes analyst evidence.
5. SLU Associate Business Analyst (https://slu.edu/business/-pdf/coverletters.pdf). Bullet-list middle section (5 bullets) in a ~170-word letter. Strong: scannable; flagged by the guide as "do not copy resume bullets". Good fallback format for short student-job letters.
6. SLU Finance Analyst Intern (same PDF). Company-specific fact in opener (firm's ranking in a named league table) plus year of study. Strong: one verifiable company fact. Weak: "renowned company... incredible first step" is cliché.
7. Yale SOM hook/sell/close lines (guide above). Hook: "My experience across professional services, marketing, and sales operations differentiates me... ability to see patterns in data that support actionable growth strategies." Strong template for a career-switcher: one sentence combining breadth + one capability.
8. Uni Graz Marketing Assistant, German (…Anschreiben.pdf, 2012 so dated). Subject line with position + Kennzahl; named addressee; first line states desire ("eine herausfordernde Tätigkeit... ist genau das, was ich suche"); thesis topic (market research) tied to the job; internship with SPSS. Strong: reference number + named person + concrete tool. Weak: soft-skill filler from call-centre and camp jobs.
9. Illinois summer internship sample (https://careercenter2022.web.illinois.edu/sites/default/files/2023-03/Sample%20Cover%20Letter-Handout.pdf). JD shown beside letter: each body paragraph answers one "desired skill" (team lead of 8 students, video editing for the website). Strong: visible JD-to-evidence mapping. Weak: "enthusiastically", "dynamic team".

## 3. Synthesis

(a) Common core (all sources). One page. Addressed to a named person where findable. Not a CV retelling: add what the CV cannot say (motivation, context, reasoning). Three questions: why this role, why this company, why me. 2-3 proofs chosen from the job's stated requirements, each with a concrete example and number. Specific, confident close with a next step. Tailored per employer (LSE swap test). No clichés or hedges. Proofread.

(b) Where they differ.
- US (MIT, Yale, Berkeley, Columbia): "Hook/Sell/Ask", story-driven opener, confident self-promotion, explicit ask (interview request, follow-up date), 4-5 paragraphs. Mention networking contact/event.
- UK (LSE, Oxford, Surrey, Essex): Why them / Why me split; 3-4 skills straight from the person specification, STAR-like evidence; understated tone, no hedges or emotive words; Yours sincerely/faithfully; short ending.
- DACH/Austria (karriere.at, AMS, news.at, StepStone, Graz): Subject line (Betreff) with title + ref no.; formal letter layout; "alles, was nicht im Lebenslauf steht"; no Konjunktiv; Gehaltsvorstellung and Eintrittstermin only on request, in the close; ad language usually sets letter language; length figures 250-400 words (StepStone/karriere) vs "one page". English applications: no photo, no personal data (AMS).
- Austria specifics. Salary: only if asked; gross annual, "14x", a range, placed at end before the sign-off (https://www.karriere.at/c/a/gehaltsvorstellung-bewerbung); avoid "branchenüblich"/"nach Vereinbarung"; employers must state a minimum in the ad, usually the collective agreement (KV) minimum - treat as a floor. Start date: only if asked, short own paragraph before last sentence (StepStone.de) or in the close (Graz). Hours/availability (student jobs): state concrete hours and days; karriere.at Nebenjob guidance puts availability as its own paragraph (https://www.karriere.at/c/a/bewerbung-nebenjob). Visa: no primary Austrian source found on mentioning it; WorkInAustria only advises explaining why you want to work in Austria. Legal context (primary, see Added sources): third-country students need an AMS work permit even for marginal jobs; permits up to 20 h/week need no labour market test; no bachelor/master split (the earlier 10/20 split is outdated). Rule: mention status in one factual sentence only if it affects employability and the status is known; otherwise omit. Need candidate's citizenship/work-authorisation status before enforcing.
- Language. WorkInAustria and AMS: German ad with no mention of English -> German letter recommended; English letter justified if the ad names English as working language, the company is international, or German is still developing (say so). Candidate decision: write English by default, but for a German-only ad add one sentence stating that applications are welcome in English / the team works in English only if verified; otherwise either produce a German version or flag the risk.

(c) Template for this candidate (English, 250-350 words, hard cap 380 after Cambridge/Stanford/DACH benchmarks; 4 short paragraphs; plain, direct, first person, no hedges).
- Header + subject line: "Application: [exact job title] ([ref no. if any])". Salutation: named person, else "Dear [Company] hiring team". Never "To whom it may concern".
- P1 Hook (40-60 words): role + where seen; one verified company-specific fact (product, launch, user segment, public metric) and why it matters to the candidate; one-sentence identity ("Product-minded BSc graduate in International Management with 3 years in Web3 products").
- P2 Proof 1 + 2 (90-120 words): pick 2 requirements from the ad (JD keywords reused); for each: situation -> action -> measurable result from the candidate's real record (Telegram media network founded and exited; Web3 product work). Name the requirement first, then the evidence. Numbers only from the supplied fact bank.
- P3 Proof 3 / gap bridge (50-80 words): third requirement, or the honest bridge for the weakest one (e.g. "no formal PM title: I ran roadmap, analytics and growth for X"). State what he will do in the first months, tied to company context.
- P4 Close (40-60 words): one concrete offer (e.g. discuss how X metric could ...), availability/start date and hours/salary range ONLY if the ad asks (salary: gross annual 14x range; student job: weekly hours + days), confident close without Konjunktiv, "Kind regards".
- German-language ad handled in order: check ad for "English" or international team -> if yes, English letter with German-matching subject line ("Bewerbung als ... / Application: ..."); if no, flag to the user: produce German version or accept lower fit; never machine-translate silently. Keep company/role names exactly as in the ad.

(d) Testable rules for a generator (each is a pass/fail check).
1. Word count 250-350; hard fail above 380; one page (Cambridge: one page is both max and min).
2. Contains exact job title and exact company name (and reference number if the ad has one); contains the subject line.
3. Contains >=1 company-specific fact with a source URL from the company's own site or press page, fetched in the same run; no URL, no fact, fail. The LSE swap test must fail (replacing the company name breaks the sentence).
4. Maps 2-3 requirements from the ad to evidence; each evidence item contains an action + a result; at least 2 of 3 carry a number or named outcome.
5. Every claim must trace to the candidate fact bank (CV + notes). No invented employers, metrics, tools, tenures or quotes; unknown -> ask or omit (MIT: AI "may even make up an experience").
6. Cliché/hedge blacklist, fail on match: "I am writing to apply", "Hiermit bewerbe ich mich", "passionate", "hard-working", "team player", "fast learner", "dynamic", "renowned", "I believe I could", "I feel that", "honoured", "excited to" (max 0), "perfect fit", "dream job", "teamfähig/belastbar/flexibel" without proof.
7. No sentence repeats a CV bullet verbatim (fuzzy match < 0.8 against the CV text); the letter must contain at least one fact not in the CV (reasoning or motivation).
8. First sentence does not start with "I" + "am writing/apply"; first 60 words contain the role, the company and the fact.
9. Reuses >=5 distinct non-generic keywords from the ad (tools, domains, methods) in natural sentences.
10. Max 1 sentence on motivation per "why" (role, company); no adjectives of emotion; no more than 25 words per sentence on average.
11. Salary, start date, weekly hours appear only if the ad requests them; format: gross annual EUR range with "14x"; weekly hours as number plus days; never "negotiable".
12. Work-authorisation sentence only if the candidate profile says it is relevant; maximum one sentence; never inferred by the generator.
13. Language check: if the ad is German and does not mention English, generator stops and asks (German version / English anyway) instead of silently producing English; if English, use British or consistent spelling, date written with month spelled out.
14. Close has one concrete next step and no Konjunktiv/hedge ("would", "hope") in the final two sentences; sign-off matches the salutation (named: "Kind regards"/"Yours sincerely"; unnamed: team address).
15. Output includes an evidence table (requirement -> sentence -> fact-bank source -> company-fact URL) for human review before sending; never auto-send (publishing as Misha is Red zone).

Suggested next step: build a fact bank file (10-15 verified evidence items with numbers), then dry-run the 15 rules on 3 real vacancies, one of them German-language.

### Changes to the synthesis
- Length: raised to 250-350 words (cap 380); Cambridge/Stanford/DACH sources converge on one full page, 3-5 paragraphs, ~250-400 words.
- Visa/work-authorisation: now has a primary precedent (Cambridge sample: one sentence "full rights to work", plus availability dates, in the close). Rule 12 stands: only if his status is known and relevant. If he is a third-country national: employers must apply for the permit, so one factual sentence helps; the weekly cap is 20 h without labour market test (not 10 h for bachelor). EU/EEA citizen: omit.
- New testable rules: (16) each paragraph's first and last sentence carries its key message (Cambridge hourglass); (17) at most 2 of 4 paragraphs start with "I"; (18) availability/start date/hours go in the closing paragraph and only when asked or relevant (Stanford, Imperial, Cambridge, Graz agree); (19) ban "I am writing to express my interest", "passionate about", "challenging opportunities", "team-based culture", "I am hereby applying" (Stanford, Imperial, UZH); (20) forbid conditional closes ("I would be happy if") per UZH/karriere.at.
- Paragraph 3 of the template (company fit) should cite a concrete, recent, public item (project, publication, news) per Stanford; this matches rule 3.
- Do not use the Stanford, UWM or SLU samples as style models; they contain the clichés the same offices now ban. Use Cambridge's annotated samples and MIT II/IV for structure.
